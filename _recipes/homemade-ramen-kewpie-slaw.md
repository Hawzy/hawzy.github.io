instructions:
  - "In a medium mixing bowl, toss the finely shredded green cabbage `2 cups` with Japanese Kewpie mayonnaise `2 tbsp` and half of the thinly sliced scallions `1 1/2 stalks` until every ribbon is glossy and evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate so the slaw stays crisp and cold until serving."
  - "Bring a medium saucepan of water to a gentle rolling boil over **medium-high heat**. Carefully lower in the cold eggs `2 large` straight from the refrigerator and cook for **6 minutes 30 seconds**. Transfer immediately to a bowl of ice water to chill for **5 minutes**, then peel and halve lengthwise to expose the jammy golden yolks."
  - "In a saucepan, combine the low-sodium chicken bone broth `4 cups`, finely grated garlic `2 cloves`, dark shoyu soy sauce `2 tbsp`, and toasted sesame oil `1 tsp`. Simmer gently over **medium heat** for **5 minutes** until nutty, deeply fragrant, and shimmering with amber sesame droplets."
  - "Bring a large pot of unsalted water to a rolling boil over **high heat**. Add the wavy ramen noodles `2 packs (5 oz each)`, stirring with chopsticks to separate the strands, and boil for **2–3 minutes** until springy and chewy (*al dente*). Drain thoroughly in a colander, shaking off excess cooking water."
  - "Divide the steaming drained noodles between two warmed bowls and ladle the piping-hot shoyu broth over the noodles until submerged."
  - "Nestle the jammy egg halves atop the noodles, scatter the remaining thinly sliced scallions `1 1/2 stalks` over the broth, and serve immediately with fresh lime `1 lime (4 wedges)` alongside the chilled crunchy Kewpie slaw."

When cold weather hits or you crave deep umami on a busy weeknight, this 30-minute shortcut ramen delivers soulful restaurant comfort without an 18-hour tonkotsu simmer.

### The 6.5-Minute Jammy Egg (*Ajitsuke Tamago* Style)
The defining hallmark of great ramen is the soft-boiled egg: firm, tender whites holding a creamy, gelatinous golden yolk. Dropping cold large eggs into actively boiling water for exactly **6 minutes and 30 seconds**, followed immediately by an aggressive ice-bath shock, halts the cook cycle at optimum texture.

### Why Kewpie Slaw?
Rich broth and chewy noodles crave a crunchy, acidic counterpoint. Japanese Kewpie mayonnaise—made exclusively with egg yolks and apple cider/rice vinegar—coats finely shaved crisp green cabbage without becoming watery, creating a cooling, creamy side salad that balances the hot ramen bowl.
