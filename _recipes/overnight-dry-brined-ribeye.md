instructions:
  - "Blot the thick-cut USDA Prime ribeye steaks `2 steaks (16 oz each)` completely dry on all faces and fat caps using clean paper towels."
  - "Season all surfaces and edges generously with coarse kosher salt `2 tsp`. Set the steaks on a wire rack nested inside a rimmed baking sheet and refrigerate uncovered for **12–24 hours** until the surface turns deep ruby and forms a dry, tacky pellicle."
  - "Remove the dry-brined steaks from the refrigerator **45 minutes** before cooking to temper on the counter, then blot away any trace surface moisture."
  - "Preheat a large heavy cast-iron skillet over **high heat** for **5 minutes** until faint wisps of smoke rise from the iron. Swirl in refined avocado oil `1 tbsp` until shimmering across the pan."
  - "Lay the steaks away from you into the smoking skillet; they should sizzle aggressively on contact. Sear undisturbed for **2 minutes** until a deep mahogany-brown crust forms and releases cleanly, then flip with tongs."
  - "Sear the second side for **2 minutes** until crisp and browned, then reduce to **medium-low heat** and immediately add unsalted European-style butter `3 tbsp`, lightly crushed garlic `4 cloves`, and fresh rosemary `2 sprigs` to the pan."
  - "Tilt the skillet slightly toward you and continuously spoon (*arroser*) the foaming, nutty browned butter over the steaks for **1.5–2 minutes** until the rosemary crackles, the garlic turns golden, and an instant-read thermometer inserted into the center registers **125°F** for medium-rare."
  - "Transfer the steaks to a warm carving board, spoon the aromatic browned butter over the crust, and rest loosely uncovered for **8 minutes** before slicing thick against the grain."

The secret to steakhouse-caliber crust and succulent, deeply seasoned beef at home is **advance dry-brining**. By salting thick-cut ribeyes 12 to 24 hours ahead and leaving them uncovered in the refrigerator, two critical culinary transformations take place:

1. **Internal Osmosis & Protein Dissolution:** The salt draws surface moisture out, dissolves into a concentrated brine, and is gradually reabsorbed into the interior muscle fibers. This denatures muscle proteins, trapping juices when exposed to high heat.
2. **Surface Dehydration:** The dry circulating refrigerator air wicks moisture from the exterior, creating a thin dry layer (pellicle). Moisture is the sworn enemy of the Maillard reaction; an ultra-dry surface browns almost instantaneously in cast iron without overcooking the delicate center.

### Finishing with *Arroser* (Butter-Basting)
Once both sides have established their hard sear, dropping the heat and adding whole butter, crushed garlic, and woody herbs initiates *arroser*—the classic French basting technique. The milk solids in European butter gently toast, bathing the steak in rich, nutty browned butter while perfuming the crust with aromatics.
