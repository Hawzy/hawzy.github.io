instructions:
  - "Place the trimmed flank steak `1 lb` in the freezer for **20 minutes** until firm to the touch but not frozen solid. Halve lengthwise with the grain if wider than 3 inches, then slice across the grain on a 45-degree bias into uniform 1/4-inch strips."
  - "In a medium bowl, combine the flank steak with baking soda `1/4 tsp`, dry Marsala wine `2 tsp`, low-sodium soy sauce `1 tbsp`, neutral oil `1 tbsp`, cornstarch `1 tbsp`, and 1 tbsp cold water. Massage for **1 minute** until absorbed into a tacky sheen, then cover and refrigerate for **30 minutes**."
  - "In a small spouted bowl, whisk warm chicken broth `1/3 cup`, light brown sugar `1/4 cup`, and unsulfured molasses `1 1/2 tsp` until fully dissolved, then stir in low-sodium soy sauce `1/4 cup` and toasted sesame oil `1 tsp`. In a separate cup, stir cornstarch `1 tbsp` with 1 tbsp cold water into a smooth slurry."
  - "Heat a 12-inch cast-iron skillet or carbon-steel wok over **high heat** until wisps of white smoke appear. Swirl in neutral oil `1 tbsp`, add the broccoli florets `1 lb`, and stir-fry briskly for **60 seconds** until bright green and spot-charred at the edges."
  - "Splash 2 tbsp cold water down the hot pan wall, cover immediately with a lid, and steam for **90 seconds** until vibrant emerald and crisp-tender. Transfer the broccoli florets to a holding plate and wipe the pan dry with paper towels."
  - "Remove the chilled flank steak from the refrigerator; if the surface feels dry rather than tacky, work in 1 tsp cold water first. Toss the strips in a bowl with cornstarch `6 tbsp` until coated in a powdery, chalky-white layer, then shake off loose excess."
  - "Heat neutral oil `6 tbsp` in the dry pan over high heat until shimmering, then lay half the flank steak flat in an uncrowded layer. Sear undisturbed for **2 minutes** until a golden-brown craggy crust sets, flip and sear for **60 seconds** until crisp, and drain on a tilted baking sheet; repeat with the remaining batch."
  - "Pour off the frying oil into a heat-safe bowl, leaving 1 tbsp oil in the pan over **medium-high heat**. Add the white onion `1 medium` petals and stir-fry for **60 seconds** until translucent at the edges yet still crisp at the core."
  - "Push the white onion to the perimeter, add fresh ginger `1 tbsp` and dried red chili peppers `4 whole` to the center oil, and bloom for **15 seconds** until fragrant. Add garlic `4 cloves` and scallion whites `6 whites` and toss together for **30 seconds** until aromatic and glossy."
  - "Pour the Mongolian glaze into the pan and simmer over medium-high heat for **60 seconds** until rapidly bubbling and slightly reduced. Re-stir the cornstarch slurry, stream it into the center while stirring constantly, and cook for **30 to 45 seconds** until thickened into a dark mahogany lacquer that coats a spoon."
  - "Increase to **high heat** and add the crispy flank steak, broccoli florets, and scallion greens `6 greens`. Toss vigorously for **30 to 45 seconds** until the scallions just wilt and every craggy ridge locks onto the glossy lacquer with no standing liquid, then serve over steamed white rice."

### Why This Method Works

Mongolian beef (*Měnggǔ niúròu*, 蒙古牛肉) originated in 1950s Taipei, Taiwan, created by crosstalk comedian Wu Zhaonan as an offshoot of griddled barbecue stir-fries before evolving into a Chinese-American restaurant classic. Done right in a home skillet or wok, it pairs tender flank steak in a crackly cornstarch crust with crisp-tender broccoli florets, sweet white onion petals, and charred scallions in a dark mahogany glaze.

Three kitchen controls keep the crust crisp and the glaze balanced:

### 1. Dual-Stage Velveting & Dry Cornstarch Crust

Lean flank steak seizes easily over high heat. Brief freezer firming makes thin 45-degree bias slicing effortless, while a small measure of baking soda raises the meat's surface pH so muscle fibers stay supple without a soapy aftertaste. Tossing the tacky marinated beef in dry cornstarch right before frying builds a craggy starch lattice that blisters crisp in oil and grips the thickened glaze.

### 2. Pantry Alchemy: Dry Marsala & Unsulfured Molasses

Two everyday pantry swaps deliver restaurant aroma and color without a specialty trip:
- **Dry Marsala wine** (or dry sherry) matches the nutty, cask-aged aromatics and alcohol content of Chinese Shaoxing wine 1-for-1 in the marinade.
- **Unsulfured molasses** (or blackstrap molasses) replaces Chinese dark soy sauce 1-for-1, staining the glaze deep mahogany and adding bittersweet caramel body without adding extra sodium.

### 3. Decoupled Wok Sequencing

Crowding raw beef and watery vegetables into a pan together drops the surface temperature and steams the starch crust into paste. Steam-blistering the broccoli florets first, shallow-frying the flank steak in two uncrowded batches, and thickening the glaze with the cornstarch slurry *before* the final toss ensures every strip stays crisp from wok to platter.
