instructions:
  - "In a small bowl, mash the softened unsalted butter `4 tbsp` with finely grated garlic `4 cloves` and fresh lemon juice `1 tsp` until smooth and pale. Keep this garlic butter beside the griddle to divide across three stages."
  - "Break up the cold cooked rice `5 cups` by hand until every grain separates. Pull the shabu-shabu beef `1 lb` into loose single ribbons, pat dry, and season with kosher salt `1/2 tsp`, black pepper `1/2 tsp`, and garlic powder `1/2 tsp`; beat the eggs `4 large` in a bowl until uniform."
  - "Preheat the griddle for **10 minutes** with the left burners on **medium-high (425°F to 450°F)** and the right burners on low (275°F to 300°F) until the left side lightly smokes."
  - "Spread neutral oil `1 tbsp` on the hot left zone, add the diced yellow onion `1 medium` and scallion whites `6 whites`, and sauté for **2 to 3 minutes** until translucent and charred at the edges."
  - "Add the frozen peas and carrots `1 1/2 cups` straight from the freezer alongside 1/2 tbsp of the garlic butter and toss for **90 to 120 seconds** until the ice evaporates and the peas turn bright green. Slide the vegetables to the low-heat right side to hold."
  - "Set the left burners to **high**, add the remaining neutral oil `1 tbsp`, and lay the seasoned shabu-shabu beef in a single uncrowded layer. Sear undisturbed for **30 to 45 seconds** until deeply browned underneath, then flip and chop into bite-sized pieces on the steel with two spatulas."
  - "Once the beef is about 80% cooked (**60 to 90 seconds** total), drizzle low-sodium soy sauce `1 tbsp` and Bachan's Japanese barbecue sauce `1 tbsp` directly over the meat. Toss for **15 seconds** until bubbling and glossy, then park the beef on top of the vegetables and scrape the hot zone clean."
  - "Lower the left burners to **medium-high (375°F to 400°F)**, melt 2 tbsp of the garlic butter, and toss the cold cooked rice to coat; fold in Kewpie mayonnaise `1 1/2 tbsp` if using. Press the rice into an even layer with the spatulas and fry undisturbed for **3 to 4 minutes** until golden, crispy bits form underneath."
  - "Push the rice outward to form a 6-inch center well, melt the remaining 1 1/2 tbsp garlic butter inside, and pour in the beaten eggs. Let them set for **20 to 30 seconds** until opaque around the edges, then gently scramble into soft, glossy curds (about 85% set), chop, and fold the rice over them."
  - "Sweep the parked beef and vegetables onto the rice and sprinkle with chicken bouillon powder `1 tsp` and white pepper `1/2 tsp`. Pour low-sodium soy sauce `1 1/2 tbsp` and Bachan's Japanese barbecue sauce `1 1/2 tbsp` over the mound and toss over **high heat** for **60 to 90 seconds** until smoky and glossy."
  - "Turn off all griddle burners. Drizzle toasted sesame oil `2 tsp` and the remaining fresh lemon juice `2 tsp` over the mound, scatter the scallion greens `6 greens` and toasted sesame seeds `1 tbsp` on top, give one final toss, and serve immediately."

Restaurant-style teppanyaki fried rice on a home flat-top comes down to three habits: keep the griddle in two heat zones, cook every component separately before it meets the rice, and save the sweet glaze for the very end.

### Use Bachan's as a Finishing Glaze

Bachan's Japanese barbecue sauce is thin like soy sauce but packs 7 to 8 grams of sugar and about 520 mg of sodium per tablespoon. Poured onto 400°F+ steel early, those sugars scorch before the rice gets crisp, and using it as the sole seasoning makes the batch overly sweet and salty. Splitting it with low-sodium soy sauce, chicken bouillon powder, and ground white pepper—and adding it only during the final 15 seconds of the steak sear and final 60 to 90 seconds of the rice toss—yields a smoky, glossy lacquer without burning.

### Two Heat Zones on the Griddle

Set the left burners to medium-high or high (425°F to 450°F) for searing and frying, and the right burners to low (275°F to 300°F) to hold finished vegetables and beef. Costco's fresh shabu-shabu beef is sliced about 1/8 inch thick, so it steams if crowded on warm steel; searing in a single layer on the smoking zone and parking it at 80% doneness keeps every ribbon tender when tossed back into the rice.

### Cold Rice and Frozen Vegetables

Day-old chilled rice fries into separate, crisp grains because starch retrogradation firms the interior while refrigerator air dries the surface. Flash-sautéing the frozen peas and carrots on bare steel before they touch the rice evaporates their surface ice crystals so steam never softens the toasted grains.
